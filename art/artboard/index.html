Apocalypse BaristaArtboard · the world asleep, and the cup that wakes it

1. The world is pastel. The actors are pencil. The coffee is light.
2. Handmade means hope. Manufactured means sleep.
3. Funny before scary. Readable before beautiful.

Style frame · San Coffesco, Market Street

The big idea in one picture. Left: the street sleeps. It is a pencil sketch under lavender haze. Right: the barista throws coffee, and color returns. The edge between the two halves is the wake front. The same front moves out from every woken sleepwalker and from every switched-off tower.

Sleep sideSaturation at 12 percent. Values pushed up toward cream and lavender. Lines stay. The world looks like an unfinished sketch, never like a horror scene.
Awake sideFull palette, warm buttery light, survivors' chalk graffiti on corporate signs. The café and the barista keep full color on the sleep side too: they never sleep.
Read order1. Barista (warmest, darkest contour). 2. Cup arc (light). 3. Sleepwalkers (lavender, arms out). 4. Haze edge. 5. Everything else.

Materials

Four dry materials on one cream paper. Texture lives on big surfaces. At character size, the silhouette wins.

GraphiteContours and the sketch layer of sleeping areas. Leave small gaps in lines where light hits.
Colored pencilForm and color of characters and props. Shadows are hatched at 45 degrees, never a smooth gradient.
Soft pastelLight pools, sky, haze, and big ground areas. Soft edges and smudges, with the paper tooth visible.
White chalkSteam, sparkle, highlights, and all interface text. It links the street to the café chalkboard.

Top row: every character as a solid shape. If you cannot name it from the shape at phone size, the design is not done. Bottom row: the color version. Sleepwalkers face right with arms out for the hug.

Do and don't

Do
  • Hatch shadows at 45 degrees, from top-right.
  • Leave small gaps in contour lines where the light hits.
  • Let the paper grain show in every flat color.
  • Give every street one small silly story.
  • Make coffee the brightest, warmest thing on the screen.
  • Test every character as a silhouette at one centimeter.
  • Test every screen in grayscale.
Don't
  • No pure black, except on Mug Roasters Co. things.
  • No smooth digital gradients or glossy highlights on world art.
  • No horror darkness, blood, or rot. Sleep is lavender, not black.
  • No dense hatching on small sprites. It turns to mud on a phone.
  • No line boil on backgrounds. It makes the screen tiring.
  • No real names or real likeness for parody characters.
